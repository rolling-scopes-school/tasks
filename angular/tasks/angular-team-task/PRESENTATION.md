# Team Presentation

**Goal:** demonstrate the product and tell the team's story — how you worked together, how you learned Angular, what decisions you made.

- **Format:** blocks of ~90 minutes, 3 teams per block, Google Meet (preferable).
- Questions: `#questions-to-admins`.

## Session plan (~30 min)

1. **Product demo (~10 min).** Show one user story from start to finish: "the user does A, then B, and gets C." Different members lead different parts of the story.
2. **Architecture, key decisions and one failure story (~10 min).** One simple diagram of the system. Two or three important decisions: what you chose, what the alternative was, and why. Then one real failure: what broke, what you tried, how it ended.
3. **Contributions (~2 min).** Each member says in 1–2 sentences what they did and what they learned.
4. **Questions (~8 min).** Questions go to the **team**, and the team decides who answers. The best pattern: pass the question to the person who built that part.

## Scoring: 100 points, one score for the team

The jury scores each point as **yes or no**. It happened, or it did not. No half points. The final score is the median of the jury scores. Every member who was present gets the team score.

1. **(10)** The demo was one user story from start to finish, not a list of features
2. **(20)** The team showed the architecture and explained at least 2 decisions with trade-offs
3. **(20)** The team told a real failure story with details
4. **(20)** Questions were answered by the whole team: for 3+ members — at least 3 different people answered; for 2 — both answered; for a solo presenter — they answered questions about _all_ parts of the system
5. **(20)** Each present member clearly said what they did. A solo presenter instead explains how they made decisions alone and what they would do differently in a team
6. **(10)** Good timing and preparation: the app worked, the demo was rehearsed
   **Small teams.** If your team now has 1 or 2 members — you present anyway, and that is respected. Solo and duo teams can be nominated for Best Project on the same terms.

## Observers

Observers may attend the sessions, including friends, team members, or mentors, etc.
Observers do not evaluate the presenter and must not interfere with the session format.

The session may only be recorded with the consent of both the presenter and the jury.

## Best Project nominations

Each juror has **1 nomination for every 3 teams they watch**. A nomination is separate from points and does not change your score. It means your project genuinely stood out — for the product, a decision, or the way you handled questions.

After all blocks, the nominations are collected and the **Best Projects list** is published, with the jury's comments on what earned the spot.

### Recommendations

- Turn on webcams if possible
- Slides are recommended but not required. All information can be in the README
- Live demo is preferred. Use video only if the live demo fails
